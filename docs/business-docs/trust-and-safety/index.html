Trust & Safety

Most Skillars users are children. This page collects every protection that follows from that — who may talk to whom, what a parent must consent to, and what happens to content before anyone sees it.

Why this is a first-class concern

Skillars puts adult coaches in contact with minors, and coaches film those minors in sessions. That combination is the single largest risk the business carries, and it shapes product decisions that otherwise look strange: why a child has no login, why minors can never upload video, why messaging needs a paid booking, why registration has four separate scroll-gated consent boxes. None of those are incidental — each is a control. This page exists so nobody removes one without understanding what it was for.

Age tiers: the spine of the whole system

Every player has a date of birth, from which the platform works out an age tier each time it is needed. The tier, not a role or a setting, decides who may take part in a conversation about that player. The age boundaries are configurable at runtime rather than hard-coded, so treat the ages below as current defaults.

TierShown asPlayer may message?Parent's role in the thread
U10Under 10NoThe parent is the participant and messages the coach on the child's behalf
AGE_10_1210–12No (parent-managed)Same as under 10: the parent is the participant
AGE_13_1713–17Yes, supervisedReads and can send in the same thread
ADULT18+YesNone. A parent cannot see or join an adult player's thread

In practice, every conversation about a minor goes through the parent's account. Minors have no login of their own: a child is a profile under a parent account. So although the 13–17 policy would let a teenager message, there is currently no way for a teenager to sign in. Only self-registered adult players have their own accounts.

The tier is calculated, never stored as a status. A player crosses a boundary silently on their birthday and their permissions change with it. Nobody is notified and there is nothing for an admin to switch, which also means no individual exception can be granted.

Consent gates at registration

Parent registration is deliberately heavier than any other flow in the product. There are four separate scroll-gated consent boxes across two pages. Each has its own checkbox, which stays disabled until that box has been scrolled to the bottom:

  1. Terms of Service — at /parent-register.
  2. Privacy Policy — at /parent-register.
  3. Parental Consent — at /parent-register.
  4. Child-specific consent — at /parent/create-player, shown only when the date of birth makes the child under 18 ("I am the legal parent/guardian and consent to…"). It is stamped with a policy version, so you can tell which wording a given parent agreed to.

Adult players who register themselves see a lighter flow with two checkboxes (Terms and Privacy). Their registration refuses any date of birth under 18 and points them to parent registration instead: self-registration is for adults only, by design.

The scroll gate is the control, not the checkbox. If someone asks to "simplify onboarding" by enabling the checkboxes immediately, they should know that the scroll requirement is what makes the consent defensible. The versioned stamp on the child consent is there for the same reason.

Messaging is gated on a real relationship

Nobody can cold-message anyone on Skillars. A conversation belongs to one coach and one player, and it can only be opened once they have a paid booking together (confirmed, upcoming, in progress or completed). A request that is still waiting for an answer doesn't count. There is no discovery messaging, and there is no way to contact someone you have never been booked with. Combined with the age tiers above, this means a coach can only reach a family they actually coach, and for a minor always through the parent.

Every message is checked by AI moderation before delivery. A message is saved as pending and becomes visible to the other side only once it has been judged safe. Messages judged unsafe are blocked. The other side never sees the content of a blocked or under-review message.

Nothing is delivered on an unresolved verdict. If the AI is unsure, the moderation service fails, or a message is still pending after 30 minutes, the message goes to a human instead of being released. The system fails closed. So a moderation outage delays messages rather than leaking unmoderated ones, and it builds up a queue that an admin clears on /admin/moderation.

Contact details: different rules for chat and for free-text fields. Chat messages are not automatically redacted. Instead, asking for personal contact details is one of the things the AI moderator blocks. Booking notes, the player's request to the coach, the coach's plan note and session recaps are scanned, and any email addresses or phone numbers are removed. Both rules exist to stop a coach and a family moving the relationship off the platform, where none of these controls apply.

Messages are deleted after 24 months, unless they are part of an open safety report, in which case they are kept as evidence.

Video: minors never upload

Video is the highest-risk content type in the product, so the rule is simple: a minor never uploads video (product decision, October 2026). The only footage of a child is a coach's session video: 

flowchart LR
    C["Coach uploads a session review"] --> B["Picks the session it is about"]
    B --> S["Automated content scanning"]
    S -->|Clean| V["Playable by the coach and by that child's parent"]
    S -->|Flagged| A["Admin video review queue"]
    S -->|CSAM match| H["Video locked, account suspended, admins alerted"]
    M["A minor tries to upload"] --> R["Refused, admin alerted"]
  1. Only a self-registered adult player can upload (homework). An upload attempt from any profile that is a minor or managed by a parent is refused before anything is stored, and an admin is alerted. This should be impossible, so if it happens, something else has broken.
  2. A coach's session review must name the session it reviews. The parent of the child in that session (or the player, if they have their own adult account) can then watch it, but cannot download or delete it.
  3. Every video is still scanned automatically before it can be played: a CSAM hash-matching check, explicit-content detection, and a minor-safety re-check that hides the video if anything looks wrong. If a child's data is erased (GDPR), the coach's videos of that child are deleted too.

A CSAM match is the most severe path in the product. The video is locked and the uploader's account is suspended immediately. If the uploader is a coach, a safety hold also follows: the profile is taken down, all their upcoming bookings are cancelled with full refunds, unused session packs are refunded, and their payouts are frozen until an admin reinstates them. A safety hold cannot be appealed. Video moderation alerts are also emailed to the admin alert address, the only alerts that are emailed.

There is no parental approval queue any more. /parent/approvals belonged to the old "minor uploads, parent approves" flow, which has been removed; the address now just opens the parent dashboard.

Reporting and enforcement

SignalRaised byWhat happens
Message reportAny party to the conversationThe message is hidden pending review and an admin alert is raised.
Conversation reportAny partyThe whole conversation is blocked for everyone immediately, then reviewed. This is the only "block" users have.
Review flagAny signed-in userOnce a review has enough flags, it is automatically held for moderation.
Reliability strikesSystem, from coach behaviourUnexcused cancellations and no-shows build up; a coach cancelling at or after the start time, or a family cancelling after the coach never started, counts as a coach no-show. At 3 the coach drops down the search results; at 5 they are hidden from search and sent to admin review, but stay bookable through a direct link by design (no automatic suspension without an admin's decision). See Money & Commercial Model.
DisputeParent, adult player or coach, within 14 days (the family also while a session awaits their confirmation)The coach's payout is frozen and the dispute goes to the admin queue for resolution. One open dispute per side of a booking — the coach's does not block the family's — and at most 2 per person per booking (disputes.maxPerBookingPerRaiser), so a dismissed dispute cannot be refiled to keep the payout frozen.
AppealThe person affected, within 14 daysA strike, coach suspension, blocked review or dispute outcome can be appealed once; the admin's decision is final.

All of these land in one admin console. Reports, AI-moderation holds, review flags, strike thresholds, disputes, GDPR deadline overruns, safety-hold bookings and payment-integrity alerts all appear in a single deduplicated queue at /admin/moderation. There are dedicated pages for disputes, appeals, coaches, videos and GDPR requests. Apart from video moderation, none of these alerts are pushed to anyone. Someone has to check the queue regularly, because the reporting tools users already have assume somebody is watching.

Data rights (GDPR)

Any user can request an export or erasure of their own data, and a parent can do the same for their child's profile. Both are real backend flows, tracked against the legal deadline on /admin/gdpr, and failed erasures are retried automatically. Two consequences are worth knowing:

  • Erasure is genuinely destructive and overrides retention protections. It permanently deletes the user's messages, even ones that ordinary retention would have kept as evidence for an open report. It also deletes reviews that hadn't been approved and anonymises approved ones. This is intentional: a right-to-erasure request wins over the moderation hold. But it means an erasure can remove material from an open investigation.
  • Coach profiles are anonymised, not deleted, so historical bookings and reviews still make sense.

Where these controls surface, by persona

Parent

Four consent gates. Takes part in every conversation about their child. Can watch the coach's session videos of their child, and can report, dispute and request GDPR actions for the child.

Player

Only adults can self-register; minors have no login at all. The age tier governs who can talk about a player.

Coach

Messaging only after a paid booking, always through the parent for minors. Reliability enforcement, appeals, and the safety-hold consequences.

Admin / Staff

Owns every queue named on this page, all in the admin console.
