Trust & Safety

Most Skillars users are children. This page collects every protection that follows from that — who may talk to whom, what a parent must consent to, and what happens to content before anyone sees it.

Why this is a first-class concern

Skillars puts adult coaches in contact with minors, then invites those minors to upload video of themselves. That combination is the single largest risk the business carries, and it shapes product decisions that otherwise look strange: why a child has no login, why messaging is booking-gated, why a parent has an approval queue, why registration has four separate scroll-gated consent boxes. None of those are incidental — each is a control. This page exists so nobody removes one without understanding what it was for.

Age tiers: the spine of the whole system

Every player has a date of birth, from which the platform derives an age tier on the fly. That tier — not a role, not a setting — determines messaging permissions and parental visibility. The boundaries are configurable at runtime rather than hard-coded, so treat the ages below as current defaults.

TierShown asCan message?Parent sees the thread?Direct coach↔player?
U10U10No — messaging prohibited entirelyYesNo
AGE_10_1210–12Yes, parent-managedYesNo
AGE_13_1713–17Yes, supervisedYesYes

Read the table as a gradient rather than four modes: as a child gets older they gain the ability to message at all (at 10), then the ability to talk to a coach directly rather than through their parent (at 13), and finally privacy from parental oversight (at 18). A parent is a visible participant in every conversation involving a minor, at every tier.

The tier is computed, never stored as a status. A player crosses a boundary silently on their birthday and their permissions change with it. There is no migration, no notification, and nothing for an admin to flip — which also means you cannot "grant" a 12-year-old direct messaging as an exception.

Under-10s cannot have an independent account at all, separately from the messaging rule. Every minor player is a profile under a parent account with no login of their own; the U10 rule additionally removes messaging even through the parent.

Consent gates at registration

Parent registration is deliberately heavier than any other flow in the product. There are four separate scroll-gated consent boxes across two pages, each with its own checkbox that stays disabled until that specific box has been scrolled to the bottom:

  1. Terms of Service — at /parent-register.
  2. Privacy Policy — at /parent-register.
  3. Parental Consent — at /parent-register.
  4. Child-specific consent — appears at /parent/create-player only when the entered date of birth computes to under 18 ("I am the legal parent/guardian and consent to…"), and is stamped with a policy version so you can tell which wording a given parent actually agreed to.

Adult self-registering players see a lighter two-checkbox flow (Terms + Privacy). Their registration also refuses any date of birth under 18, redirecting to the parent registration page instead — self-registration is adults-only by design.

The scroll gate is the control, not the checkbox. If someone asks to "simplify onboarding" by enabling the checkboxes immediately, understand that the scroll requirement is what makes the consent defensible. The versioned stamp on the child consent exists for the same reason.

Messaging is gated on a real relationship

Nobody can cold-message anyone on Skillars. A conversation can only exist between a coach and a player who have an actual booking relationship — there is no coach-to-parent inbox, no discovery messaging, and no way to contact someone you have never been booked with. Combined with the age tiers above, this means a coach cannot reach a child they have not been booked with, and for under-13s cannot reach them directly at all.

On top of that, message content is run through AI moderation before delivery. A message is written as pending and only becomes visible once a verdict comes back. Blocked and under-review messages never have their content returned to either party.

Nothing is delivered on an unresolved verdict. If moderation fails or never returns, the message is escalated for a human decision rather than released — the system fails closed. The business consequence is that a moderation outage delays messages rather than leaking unmoderated ones, and produces a queue of items an admin must clear. There is currently no admin screen to clear that queue — see Admin / Staff.

Contact details are stripped automatically. Free text is scanned for email addresses and phone-number-shaped strings and redacted. The point is to stop a coach and a minor moving the relationship off the platform, where none of these controls apply.

Video: the parental approval gate

Video is the highest-risk content type in the product, and it has the most layers between upload and visibility: 

flowchart LR
    U["Minor uploads a video"] --> S["Automated content scanning"]
    S --> P["/parent/approvals
Parent approves or rejects"]
    P -->|Approved| V["Usable / visible"]
    P -->|Rejected| X["Not published"]
    S -->|Flagged| A["Admin moderation queue"]
  1. Uploaded video is scanned automatically, including a CSAM hash-matching check and explicit-content detection, before it can become publicly available.
  2. For a minor, the video additionally lands in their parent's approval queue at /parent/approvals, showing the player, video type and date with Approve/Reject actions. This is the minor-safety gate: a child's video does not become usable until their parent has said so.
  3. An adult self-registered player is not subject to the parental gate, but is still subject to scanning.

/parent/approvals means video approvals, not booking approvals — a naming trap worth knowing before you go looking for it, and a common source of confusion when explaining the product.

Reporting and enforcement

SignalRaised byWhat happens
Message reportEither party to a conversationMessage moves to under-review; an admin alert is raised.
Conversation reportEither partyThe whole conversation is blocked immediately, then reviewed.
Review flagCommunityEnough flags auto-hold a published review pending moderation.
Reliability strikesSystem, from coach behaviourLate cancellations and no-shows accumulate; thresholds reduce marketplace visibility, then suspend the coach pending review. See Money & Commercial Model.
DisputeParent or coach, over a bookingEnters the dispute queue for admin resolution.

Every one of these lands in a queue with no admin UI. Reports, flags, strike thresholds and disputes all raise real, deduplicated admin alerts in the backend — and there is no screen to read or action them. Anyone planning a launch should treat "build the moderation console" as a safety prerequisite rather than a nice-to-have, because the reporting mechanisms users can already reach assume somebody is watching.

Data rights (GDPR)

Parents and adult users can request data export or erasure. Both are real, time-boxed backend flows. Two consequences worth knowing when discussing them:

  • Erasure is genuinely destructive and overrides retention protections. It hard-deletes the user's messages even where ordinary retention would have preserved them as evidence of an open report, and deletes non-approved reviews while anonymising approved ones. This is intentional — a right-to-erasure request wins over the moderation hold — but it means an erasure can remove material from an open investigation.
  • Coach profiles are anonymised, not deleted, so historical bookings and reviews stay coherent.

Where these controls surface, by persona

Parent

Four consent gates, the video approval queue, visibility into every conversation involving their child.

Player

Adults-only self-registration; minors have no login at all. Age tier governs what a player can do.

Coach

Booking-gated messaging, age-tier limits on contacting minors, and reliability enforcement.

Admin / Staff

Owns every queue named on this page — and currently has a UI for none of them.
