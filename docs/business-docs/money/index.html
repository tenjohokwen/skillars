Money & Commercial Model

Every rule that moves money in Skillars, in one place — how a session gets paid for, what the platform takes, and what happens on cancellation, expiry, and refund.

Figures on this page are the values currently seeded in configuration. Nearly all of them are runtime-tunable by an admin without a redeploy, so treat them as current defaults rather than constants — but they are the numbers the product ships with today.

The one path money takes

There used to be several. There is now exactly one route from a parent's card to a coach's bank account, and understanding it explains most of the product's commercial behaviour: 

flowchart LR
    A["Coach publishes ONE
session pack"] --> B["Parent saves a card"]
    B --> C["Parent buys the pack
(charged up front)"]
    C --> D["Parent holds credits
with that coach"]
    D --> E["Parent requests a booking
spending one credit"]
    E --> F["Coach accepts"]
    F --> G["Stripe destination charge
coach payout + platform commission"]

No pack means no bookings. Credits are the only entry point to a booking, and credits come only from a coach's published pack. A coach who leaves the pack step of their profile builder empty is listed in the marketplace but cannot be booked by anyone. This is the most common cause of "I'm live but getting no requests" — see Coach.

Pausing a pack

A parent can pause a pack to stop the expiry clock — for an injury or a holiday — with tight limits:

  • Once per pack lifetime. There is no second pause, ever.
  • Maximum pause length is 90 days by default.
  • The pause must start today or later, and must start before the pack expires.
  • Expiry moves out by exactly the pause length.
  • Conflicting bookings must be resolved first. If any of the parent's bookings with that coach fall inside the pause window, the first attempt returns that list without applying the pause; the parent must explicitly confirm cancelling them before it commits. This two-step confirm is intended behaviour, not an error state.

What the platform takes

ChargeDefaultApplies to
Platform commission8%Deducted from each session payment as the application fee on the Stripe destination charge — so the split happens in a single Stripe call rather than an invoice back to the coach.
Stripe processing fee2.5% + 0.25Stripe's own cut. Also charged separately to a parent cashing out credit.

A coach's revenue dashboard shows gross earnings with both deducted, so the figure they see is real take-home. Coaches cannot be paid at all until Stripe Connect onboarding is complete — an incomplete Stripe account is the most common reason a payment fails at booking time.

Credit wallet

Separately from coach-scoped session credits, a parent holds a cash balance with Skillars. This is where refunds and adjustments land — money is never silently returned to a card.

  • The ledger is append-only: a correction is a new reversing entry, never an edit. This means the statement at /parent/credit-statement is a true audit trail.
  • Balance can be spent on future bookings, or cashed out back to the parent's card minus the Stripe processing fee.
  • Where a booking is part-covered by wallet balance, credit is applied first and only the remainder is charged to the card — a parent with enough balance never touches Stripe for that booking.

Subscriptions

Two entirely separate subscription products, easily confused because both are called "tiers":

Coach subscriptions

SCOUT → INSTRUCTOR → ACADEMY. Paid by the coach to unlock platform capability. The gating is real, not marketing: a coach on the entry SCOUT tier sees a locked overlay instead of the session builder.

Player subscriptions

ATHLETE → SEMI_PRO → PRO. Bought by a parent for a child (or by an adult player), unlocking video and skills-radar capability. SEMI_PRO and PRO are yearly billing only.

Upgrades and downgrades are deliberately asymmetric. An upgrade applies immediately with proration. A downgrade is scheduled and only takes effect at the end of the current billing period — the customer keeps what they paid for. A failed payment moves the subscription to past-due and, after a grace period, downgrades it automatically.

"Excused" coach cancellations — mutual agreement, health/medical, family emergency, and weather — still refund the parent in full but do not count against the coach. A refunded pack session is returned to the pack; a refunded cash payment lands in the credit wallet.

Reliability strikes

Unexcused coach cancellations and no-shows accumulate strikes on a rolling 30-day window, with two consequences:

  • 3 strikes → marketplace visibility reduced.
  • 5 strikes → coach suspended pending admin review.

This is the platform's main lever over supply-side behaviour. Coaches see their own strike history and can acknowledge strikes at /coach/reliability. Because strikes roll off after 30 days, a coach can recover without intervention — but a suspension needs an admin to lift it, and there is no admin UI for that today.

Gotchas worth knowing before you quote a number

Forfeiture is real and it is silent. Between per-pack expiry, sub-6h cancellations, and player no-shows, a parent can lose paid-for sessions in three different ways. Only pack expiry emails them. This is the most likely source of billing complaints, and it is worth being straight about in marketing copy.

A card must be saved before any purchase. Session-pack purchase and player subscriptions both disable their confirm button until a card is on file. A parent who expects to enter card details at checkout will hit a gate earlier than they expect.

Batch bookings don't draw on a pack. When a parent selects several slots at once, the batch settles against the credit wallet/card rather than pack credits. Only single booking requests offer a pack selector.

Config changes are not instant. Commission rate, strike thresholds and pack limits are all admin-tunable at runtime, but the value is cached for around five minutes. A rate change that "didn't work" has usually just not refreshed yet.
