Skillars — Business Documentation

What Skillars is, who it's for, how it makes money, and exactly how to execute each persona's real flows in the live product.

What Skillars is

Skillars is a marketplace that connects private football/soccer coaches with players for one-on-one and small-group training, built around a Parent account that supervises and pays for a minor child's participation. Coaches list themselves publicly, publish availability, and get booked and paid through the platform; players (adult self-registered, or added as a child profile by a parent) receive assigned homework drills, get assessed on a multi-skill "Skills Radar," and can upload video for review.

The four personas

Player

The athlete being coached — self-registers if 18+, otherwise added by a parent with no login of their own.

Parent

The account that books, pays, and consents on behalf of a minor child — Skillars' primary paying customer.

Coach

The paid supplier — publishes a profile, sets availability, delivers sessions, plans training content, gets paid.

Admin / Staff

Internal operations — infrastructure health and B2B partner management today; moderation/disputes/GDPR are real but not yet in the UI.

How the business makes money

  • Per-session and session-pack payments from parents (or players), paid up front as pay-per-session or a discounted multi-session bundle.
  • Commission on coach earnings — a coach's revenue dashboard shows gross earnings with commission and Stripe processing fees deducted, meaning Skillars takes a cut of every paid session.
  • Coach subscriptions — coaches sit on tiers (a "Scout" entry tier is visibly feature-gated out of parts of the product, e.g. the session builder), implying paid tier upgrades unlock more capability.
  • Player/parent subscriptions — a separate recurring subscription a parent can buy for a child, distinct from one-off session packs.
  • Credit wallet — parents hold a cash balance usable across bookings, with the ability to cash out unused funds.

What's not built yet (read this before promising a flow)

Player self-booking — the marketplace "book" call-to-action only functions for Parent accounts.

Parent's central dashboard (/parent/dashboard) is a placeholder stub — the real parent experience lives on separate bookings/wallet/development pages reached by direct navigation.

Review submission — coach ratings display on profiles, but no screen in the current UI lets anyone actually submit one.

Admin moderation, disputes, GDPR requests, and coach enforcement are real backend capabilities with no admin UI yet — see Admin / Staff.
