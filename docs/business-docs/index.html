Skillars — Business Documentation

What Skillars is, who it's for, how it makes money, and exactly how to execute each persona's real flows in the live product.

What Skillars is

Skillars is a marketplace that connects private football/soccer coaches with players for one-on-one and small-group training, built around a Parent account that supervises and pays for a minor child's participation. Coaches list themselves publicly, publish availability, and get booked and paid through the platform; players (adult self-registered, or added as a child profile by a parent) receive assigned homework drills, get assessed on a multi-skill "Skills Radar," and can upload video for review.

The four personas

Player

The athlete being coached — self-registers if 18+, otherwise added by a parent with no login of their own.

Parent

The account that books, pays, and consents on behalf of a minor child — Skillars' primary paying customer.

Coach

The paid supplier — publishes a profile, sets availability, delivers sessions, plans training content, gets paid.

Admin / Staff

Internal operations — only the health dashboard genuinely works today; moderation, disputes, enforcement and GDPR are real backend capability with no UI.

How the business makes money

  • Session-pack payments from parents, paid up front. A coach publishes one active pack (a session count at a total price); the parent buys it, then spends those credits on bookings. This is now the only way money enters the platform for coaching — see Money & Commercial Model.
  • Commission on coach earnings — a coach's revenue dashboard shows gross earnings with commission and Stripe processing fees deducted, meaning Skillars takes a cut of every paid session.
  • Coach subscriptions — coaches sit on tiers (a "Scout" entry tier is visibly feature-gated out of parts of the product, e.g. the session builder), implying paid tier upgrades unlock more capability.
  • Player/parent subscriptions — a separate recurring subscription a parent can buy for a child, distinct from one-off session packs.
  • Credit wallet — parents hold a cash balance usable across bookings, with the ability to cash out unused funds.

An adult self-registered Player can now book sessions independently, using the same session-pack purchase and booking-request flows as Parents.

The commercial path is now pack-first, and that has a hard consequence. A parent can only request a booking once they hold session credits with that coach, and credits only come from buying that coach's published session pack. A coach who never defines a pack in step 3 of their profile builder is effectively unbookable — the marketplace still lists them, but the parent's only call-to-action leads to a purchase page reading "This coach has no session pack available for purchase." Worth checking first whenever someone reports "nobody can book me."

What's not built yet (read this before promising a flow)

Admin moderation, disputes, GDPR requests, and coach enforcement are real backend capabilities with no admin UI yet — see Admin / Staff.

The /admin/tenants screens are broken, not merely unfinished. The B2B partner feature was removed from the backend, but its frontend pages, routes, and API calls were left in place. They now call endpoints that no longer exist. See Admin / Staff.

Previously listed here and now resolved:

  • /parent/dashboard is no longer a placeholder stub — it is a real hub with player cards and live quick-link tiles. See Parent.
  • Review submission — Parents and Players can now submit, edit, and manage reviews on coach profiles. See Coach.
  • Player self-booking — Adult self-registered Players can now purchase session packs and request bookings independently. See Player.
